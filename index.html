About Me

Algorithms, Security, Systems & Sensing

I'm interested in how we reason about complex problems, build systems to investigate them, and explain what we learn. In ASU's C.O.M.P.A.S.S. Research Group, I work with Dr. Nariman Mahabadi on subsurface anomaly detection, combining field instrumentation, reproducible Python pipelines, spectrograms, and convolutional neural networks. My algorithms work uses chess to explore large state spaces, pruning, and time-space tradeoffs.

Teaching is a big part of that work. I support ASU students in Introduction to Cybersecurity and Operating Systems, building on experience teaching mathematics, engineering, photography, and chess. My background in film and television editing still shapes how I communicate technical ideas. Long term, I want to pursue research and teaching in algorithms, security, systems, and computational methods.

Research & Publications

Current Focus

Acoustic Sensing & Machine Learning

An end-to-end pipeline for subsurface anomaly detection in sand and soil: controlled acoustic signals, geophone recordings, preprocessing, spectrogram datasets, and CNN classification.

CSV-driven Python tooling supports waveform-generator control, batch WAV augmentation, object-presence classification, geophone-placement analysis, and wave-frequency estimation. Experiments integrate Zoom F3 recordings and ROS 2 rover / UGV platforms.

Chess & Computational Complexity

Recursive legal-move generation and pruning to enumerate reachable positions and reduce redundant traversal. Compared dictionary, set, trie, and list-based storage and duplicate detection through search depth 5.
